FIXYACHIPS METHOD - WHY REPAIR YOUR WINDSHIELD

FINANCIAL & INSURANCE BENEFITS

Q: How much does windshield repair cost compared to replacement?
A: Repair typically costs between $70 and $95, compared to $300 to $1,500 or more for a new windshield. You save hundreds of dollars by repairing instead of replacing.

Q: Will my insurance cover windshield repair?
A: Most insurance companies waive your comprehensive deductible for a repair, making it $0 out of pocket. Small glass repair claims rarely trigger premium increases unlike major replacement claims.

Q: What is ADAS recalibration and why does it matter?
A: Replacing a modern windshield requires recalibrating forward-facing cameras and sensors, which often adds $250 to $600 or more to the bill. Repair avoids this cost entirely.

Q: How does repair protect my vehicle resale value?
A: Buyers and appraisers prefer vehicles with their original factory glass intact. A repaired windshield preserves that original glass and protects your resale value.

Q: What happens if I ignore a small chip?
A: Fixing a $75 chip today stops it from turning into a $1,000 crack tomorrow. Temperature changes, car washes, and door slams can spread a chip into a full crack instantly.

Q: Can repair save my window tint?
A: Yes. If you paid for ceramic or UV-blocking windshield tint, a repair saves you from paying to re-tint new glass after a replacement.

Q: What about leased vehicles?
A: Leasing companies charge steep fees for unrepaired glass damage upon return. Repair protects you from those turn-in penalties.

Q: Are there hidden costs with replacement?
A: Yes. Replacements often carry disposal fees, shop supply fees, urethane kit charges, new rubber gaskets, clips, cowls, and potential recalibration appointments. Repair has none of these hidden costs.

Q: What about specialty windshields like HUD or heated glass?
A: HUD-compatible windshields have specialized coatings that are costly to replace. Windshields with built-in wiper de-icers or heating grids are notoriously expensive to swap out. Repair saves all of that.

SAFETY & STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY

Q: Is a repaired windshield as safe as the original?
A: High-grade acrylic resin bonds the glass layers back together and restores structural strength to the damaged area. Nothing matches the robotic factory-applied bond, which is exactly why preserving it through repair is always the safer choice.

Q: How does the windshield affect my car's safety in a crash?
A: In a rollover accident the windshield provides up to 60% of the cabin's structural support. Passenger-side airbags also bounce off the windshield at high speed. The factory seal must hold for both systems to work correctly.

Q: Can a chip affect my ADAS safety systems?
A: Yes. Lane-keep assist, automatic emergency braking, and adaptive cruise control all rely on a camera mounted to the windshield. Repair keeps that mount undisturbed and those systems properly aligned.

Q: What happens if moisture gets into a chip?
A: Water trapped inside a chip delaminated the inner PVB plastic safety layer and expands when it freezes, splitting the glass wide open. Sealing the chip immediately stops both problems.

Q: Can an unrepaired chip cause sun glare?
A: Absolutely. Unrepaired chips refract sunlight and oncoming headlights directly into the driver's eyes and can cause a spreading crack to unexpectedly shoot across your direct line of sight at highway speeds.

ENVIRONMENTAL & SUSTAINABILITY

Q: Are old windshields recyclable?
A: Rarely. Because auto glass is laminated with a polyvinyl butyral PVB plastic interlayer, most windshields end up in landfills. Every repaired windshield keeps roughly 30 pounds of laminated glass waste out of the dump.

Q: How is repair better for the environment?
A: Making new automotive float glass requires melting silica at over 2,700 degrees Fahrenheit. Repair uses only a UV curing lamp that consumes negligible electricity. Repair also eliminates heavy wooden shipping crates, cardboard, foam, freight fuel, and chemical waste from urethane tubes and primers.

Q: Does repair support sustainability goals for fleets?
A: Yes. Corporate fleets lower their environmental impact metrics significantly by choosing repair over replacement across multiple vehicles.
